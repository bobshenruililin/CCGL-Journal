# One route, rewritten as a testable specimen

**Proposal, not integrated app content.** Route 1–1–2: take the edit; check the room assumption; invite Dev's bounded expertise. This invented revision tests economy and causal continuity. A live-call slot and the last shared rehearsal are new fictional constraints, not newly discovered facts. It does not claim course validation.

## Tuesday · 10:40 PM

Eighteen slides. Seven minutes. On Friday, we ask the campus panel to investigate a two-week trial of later study-room hours. They can recommend a review, not approve the money.

Jules's interview notes cover the table.

“Every time I cut a quote, it sounds like we're speaking for them.”

My phone lights up. Dev: “Tomorrow is our last slot together. I leave at 5:40. Work at six.”

You are Mina. What do you say?

- “Send me the file. I'll reshape it tonight.”
- “Three slides together. Then we split the rest.”
- “Midnight, not two. Let's make the smaller case.”

### The line I chose

“Send me the file. I'll reshape it tonight.”

By 1:18, eighteen slides have become eight.

In the morning, Jules puts a sentence beside my laptop.

“I'd stay late **if the buses still ran**.”

In my version, the last five words are gone.

“I don't need every word,” Jules says. “I need that bit.”

I put it back.

*Object direction:* Show original and shortened slips together. The condition stays readable without unfolding or animation. The correction remains on Wednesday's page.

## Wednesday · 5:10 PM

The corrected quote is still beside my laptop.

Jules points to the map. “They asked for somewhere to study. Did anyone say this room?”

One interviewee can talk now, before their shift. Jules can call, but they'll miss our last full rehearsal.

Dev puts the orange timer on the table. Thirty minutes.

- “Take the call. We'll agree what to say if we learn nothing.”
- “Let's rehearse. Put the unanswered question on the page.”
- “Ten minutes to argue it out, then we vote. Keep the dissent.”

### The line I chose

“Take the call. We'll agree what to say if we learn nothing.”

We write the fallback before Jules steps outside: *Demand for this room is unconfirmed.*

Dev and I practise the handover twice.

Jules comes back as he packs his bag. “Maybe, if the late bus stops nearby.”

One person. One condition. We leave the fallback in.

*Object direction:* The timer and the bus condition belong in the same composition. Time was spent to learn something that makes the request less certain, not to earn a reassuring answer. Do not turn the conditional reply into a green success stamp.

## Friday · 9:07 AM

Jules waits half a beat at the handover. I slide my notes across. We find the next line.

A panel member leans forward. “What would the extra staffing cost?”

We have no estimate. Dev checked the room rules, not the staffing rates.

- “We don't have a figure. Can the review establish one?”
- “Dev, can you explain the rules we do know?”
- “We'll contact facilities and send an update on Monday.”

### The line I chose

“Dev, can you explain the rules we do know?”

“That part,” I add. “We haven't got the costs.”

Dev puts both hands on the table.

“The current rules don't tell us who would need to stay. Facilities would have to answer that.”

The panel member writes *staffing* beside *late transport*.

Neither has disappeared. Neither belongs to Dev alone.

*Object direction:* The unanswered questions remain visible on the paper. Moving the microphone is not a magical transfer of knowledge. Preserve the pause without forcing a delay before input.

## Afterwards

Outside, Dev puts the orange timer between us, switched off.

Jules's phone lights up. They turn it over.

We can give each other this minute.

**What would you check before handing your next piece of work to someone else?**

*Optional depth:* Keep the original route's fuller gains, costs and perspectives available in a reader-controlled margin, but test the scene without them first. This specimen intentionally removes the three concluding moral summaries. Ask a fresh reader what they understood before deciding whether to restore any of them.

## What this specimen does not settle

It does not prove that all three opening alternatives are equally appealing, that a last shared rehearsal is credible without more character context, or that the shortened prose feels emotionally sufficient. Those are review questions. The new shared premise and changed scope option require a full nine-choice consistency pass before integration. No new art or live interface is claimed.
