# Make the stories visible

## The problem (your feedback, and you're right)

Each of you tells a story from your own life: friends going quiet, a group project, a debate, a dance team. But the screen shows notebook footage of the game the whole time. The ear hears four different lives; the eye sees one repeated interface. So the sections blur together, and the link between *your life* and *the game*, which is the point of the film, is never shown, only said.

## The move: your life in pencil, then the game

While each of you tells your personal story, the footage gives way to an **animated pencil sketch of that story**, drawn line by line on notebook paper, in the game's own ink colours. A small header says whose life it is. When the speaker says "in the game", the game footage returns.

Every section now has a shape the viewer can feel: **in my life → in the game → the lesson.**

| Section | What the speaker says | What the pencil draws |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Bob, Monday** | "friends go quiet… everyone assumed they'd stopped caring. It was family. A breakup. Things none of us could see." | A group chat on a phone. One friend's messages fade to grey: *read, no reply*. Then the phone turns over, and behind it are the things nobody saw: a house (*family*), a broken heart (*a breakup*), and a fog over the rest. |
| **Kate, Thursday** | "people were busy and missing meetings… I quietly took their work on myself… a teammate told me, really gently, to stop carrying it all." | One figure. Pages labelled with teammates' tasks drop onto her pile one by one until she bends under it. A second figure arrives and takes half. |
| **Hannah, Friday** | "my debate topic was democratic versus commanding leadership… I thought one of them had to be better." | Two podiums, DEMOCRATIC and COMMANDING, and a set of scales between them that tips firmly to one side. Then a question mark. |
| **Amanda, Friday night** | "a dance team where everyone had different training and styles… by highlighting each person's strengths, it came out even more unique." | Five dancers in grey pencil, each labelled with a style. On "strengths", each fills with a different colour, one at a time. It's the same idea as the game's colour painting, which follows straight after. |
| **Bob, Saturday** | "Someone once told me to increase my surface area: try more things." | A small circle labelled *me*. It grows outwards in rings, picking up the things we tried: debate, dance, a game. |

These give the film five new visual scenes, about 45 seconds in total, all in one consistent hand-drawn style. Kaptainkristian and Every Frame a Painting do the same: the visuals carry the idea, not just illustrate it.

## Also fixing

1. **The last question.** "What is it like to be led by you?" was pale yellow handwriting over a darkened cream page, which made it hard to read. It now gets its own screen: near-black, large serif type in white, nothing else.
2. **Amanda's 3D flight comes first.** It moves to the start of her section, where she describes "the painting at the very end". It's a better fit, and the sketch then takes over for her dance-team story.
3. **Less repetition.** The five lessons appeared three times: on lesson cards, in the finale, and again on the "Play them" card. The end card now shows just the three games, so the finale is the only recap.

## Not changing

The spoken words. They're your recordings, and cutting sentences I can't hear risks clumsy edits. If, after watching this version, a section still feels long, tell me which one and I'll cut it at a sentence boundary.

## A fresh pair of eyes

A new session would need about an hour just to learn the pipeline before it could change anything, so I'll keep building here. For the review, the best fresh eyes are human: one person who hasn't seen any draft watches it once and tells you what the film is about. If they say "leadership is what your choices do to other people", it works.
