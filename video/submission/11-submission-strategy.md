# Submission strategy: one link, watch then play

## The framing

**One product, two parts.** The film carries the argument and the game is the evidence. Six Flights is the deliverable, and it opens on the film:

1. **Watch** (5:25). Our reflection film on building three leadership games. Each of us tells a story from our own life, sees it drawn, follows it into the game, and names the lesson.
2. **Play** (10 min). The game the film is about. No score; the people you led write the feedback.

Call it the **reflection film**, not the "process video". "Process video" sounds like a supplement. The film is the part every marker will definitely watch, so it has to stand on its own, and it does.

Why this order works for the three marks:
- **Mastery:** the film ties each lesson to a course session, and the information sheet shows one situation rebuilt three times as our understanding changed.
- **Communication:** five minutes, one argument, the course's own question at both ends.
- **Creativity:** a playable game with 27 routes, three builds, hand-drawn sequences and a 3D opening.
