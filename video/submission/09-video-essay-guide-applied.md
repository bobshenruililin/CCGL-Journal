# The video-essay guide, applied honestly

The guide is good. It's written for an **eight-minute design documentary about building a game**. Ours is a **five-and-a-half-minute leadership film for a course**, marked on mastery of the topic, communication and creativity. So I took the principles that serve that brief and left the ones that would turn it into a devlog.

## Already in the film (the guide's core advice)

| Principle | Where it is |
| --- | --- |
| **Open on a question the viewer understands at once** (Every Frame a Painting, Nerdwriter) | The course's own question, lifted off the real Moodle page: *Do you fit your definition of leadership?* |
| **Show what you made early** (Game Maker's Toolkit) | The game is on screen by 0:16 and named by 0:29. |
| **Comparison the viewer can judge before you explain it** (Every Frame a Painting, Now You See It) | Two split screens: the same Monday choice (knock / ask the group), and the same direct style on Wednesday and Friday. |
| **One visual model, returned to** (Estelle Caswell) | The notebook. Every section is a page of it; every transition is a page turn. |
| **A recurring object that opens and closes the film** (the guide's own advice for your film) | An answer typed into a journal. At the start it's easy and abstract. At the end, on the game's last page, it's concrete: *"Next term I ask before I carry."* |
| **Intermediate states, not just the result** (Sebastian Lague) | Three builds: Team Quest scored you; Six Flights took the score away; Asterwind put you in the room. |
| **A visual identity built from the subject** (kaptainkristian) | Notebook paper, handwriting, cut-outs with a paper edge, page turns. No borrowed motion-graphics template. |
| **One complete interaction the viewer can follow** | Monday's knock: the choice, the door, Kit's answer, uninterrupted. |
| **Connect the game to a larger human question** (Jacob Geller) | Every section starts from a real story in the speaker's own life: friends going quiet, a teammate carrying chairs, a debate, a dance team. |

## Added in this pass

| Principle | Change |
| --- | --- |
| **Music enters for a reason and stops when something deserves attention** | The music enters on the title. It now **drops away** before "What is it like to be led by you?", so the question lands in near-silence, then comes back under the last page. |
| **J-cut: sound leads the picture** | The rain starts under the journal page, a beat before we cut to the desk. |
| **Keep the people's personality in the visuals** | The cut-outs with a paper edge are back, on the chapter cards and the title. |

## What the guide would still ask for, and what I'd do about it

1. **Real people on screen, making a decision while it's uncertain** (Noclip). This is the biggest gap. Our only people are cut-outs and voices. **Ten seconds of the four of you at a laptop, arguing over a choice or laughing at a result, filmed on a phone, would do more than any effect I can add.** If you send it, it goes behind "Our answer was to build a game" in the opening.
2. **A newcomer playing it** (Razbuten). One classmate, filmed for 20 seconds as they reach the Saturday page and read what their teammates wrote. This would be the best evidence in the film that the game works.
3. **The guide's eight-minute structure** (first attempt, then the observation that challenged it, then the redesign). I'd **not** restructure now. The TA's limit is "five minutes and something", it's due tomorrow, and the marks are for leadership, not game development. The three-builds section already tells the attempt-and-revise story in thirty seconds.

Both clips are optional. The film stands without them.
