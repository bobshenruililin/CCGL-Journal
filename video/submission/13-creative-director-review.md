### The three strongest critiques from a skeptical classmate

**Of the game:**
1. "It's ten minutes of reading with three clicks." True: three decisions a week, and the rest is reading.
2. "It tells you the moral." The lessons are named on cards, so the game is less of a discovery than it claims.
3. "No score, no failure. Why would I replay 27 weeks?" Only Saturday gives a reason to replay.

**Of the video:**
1. "It's five mini-presentations with the same shape." v4 breaks the pattern only at the start and the end.
2. "Lots of polish, no evidence." Nobody outside the group is ever seen playing it.
3. "It's long." 5:23 against a suggested 3–4 minutes.

### Hidden assumptions, and whether they've been tested outside the team

| Assumption | Tested outside the team? |
| --- | --- |
| Players read every line | No |
| Players connect Friday's outcome to Monday's choice | No. The film has to explain it, which suggests it isn't obvious. |
| A game with no score is still engaging | No |

| Leadership is mainly about care for the team. The course also covers vision, ethics, and decisions under uncertainty. | It's a framing choice, never examined. |

**Finding:** there is no outside data at all. The game stores nothing, Vercel Web Analytics is off, and no recorded playtest exists. This is the echo chamber, confirmed.

### One outside test you can still run (Sunday morning, about 45 minutes)

Three people who haven't seen the game each play Monday to Saturday on a phone, about 10 minutes each. Don't help them. Afterwards ask three questions, and write the answers down word for word:

1. "In one sentence, what is this game?"
2. "What happened on Friday, and why?"
3. "Would you play again? What would you choose differently?"

Also note where they skimmed and where they hesitated.

**How to show it honestly:** replace the middle beat of "The honest part" with one real quote and one change it would make. Or better, Bob records the 25-second version below. Use first names or "a friend", ask their permission, and keep their faces off camera unless they agree.

**Bob's script (about 25 seconds), once the test is done:**

> Here's the honest part. Every lesson in this game came from our own lives, so until this weekend the only people who'd really played it were us. Then three friends played it cold. [What they said, word for word.] That told us what doesn't work yet: [the weakness they found]. Next, we want to [the change].

## 6. Why not under 5:00

Getting under 5:00 means cutting about 25 seconds. That would take one person's story, or the bookend and the hook together. I'd rather spend 5:23 on four complete stories than 4:59 on three. Your TA accepted "5 minutes and something". If a hard 5:00 cap matters, the cleanest cut is Hannah's section from "I really like that the game never tells you…" to the end of that sentence (about 4 s), plus 3 s of credits: about 5:16, still not 5:00. Cutting further means dropping a story.
