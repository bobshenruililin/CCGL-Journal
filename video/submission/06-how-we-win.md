# How we win: an honest comparison with *Rejected*, and the moves that close the gap

I looked at getrejected.co on desktop and phone. It's very good. Here is where it beats us, where we beat it, and what to do about each, against the three marking thirds: **mastery of the topic, quality of communication, evidence of creativity.**

## Where *Rejected* is stronger

1. **You get it in three seconds.** "Get rejected. On purpose." One line, one promise, one mechanic. We have no line like that. A viewer needs our whole first minute to understand what Six Flights is.
2. **It feels like a real product.** Clean black-and-white design, a scroll-driven story about Buddy, levels, a FAQ, a waitlist. It looks like something you could ship tomorrow.
3. **Its story is one arc.** Buddy's 21 days run from fear to the door he finally walks through. It is simple and satisfying.
4. **It asks something of you in real life**, not on a screen.

## Where we are stronger

1. **It's leadership, not self-help.** *Rejected* trains your own courage. Six Flights is about what your decisions do *to other people*: who carries the chairs, who leaves the group, who gets colour. That is the course's subject. This is our biggest edge, and right now we never say it out loud.
2. **You can play it today.** *Rejected* is a waitlist. Six Flights is live, free, and runs on a phone in ten minutes, with 27 different weeks.
3. **It's built from the course.** Each of our five lessons comes from a specific lecture or case. Theirs doesn't cite the course on the page.
4. **The team led itself.** Four voices, four personal stories, three games built in sequence, a teammate's prototype that shaped the next. The video *is* evidence of leadership, not just about it.
5. **The craft.** Painted art, a living notebook, page turns, a 3D camera through a painting, every number checkable.

## Bet hard

**Our one sentence: leadership is what your choices do to other people.** Every part of the submission should say it:

- **Video:** the cold open asks "What do you do?" and answers "Every choice lands on somebody." Keep it as the spine.
- **Information sheet:** the first line of "Why this topic" becomes: *"Most tools for practising leadership score the leader. We wanted one that shows you the people who pay for your choices."*
- **Site:** the cover line, above.
- **End card:** "What is it like to be led by you?" stays.
