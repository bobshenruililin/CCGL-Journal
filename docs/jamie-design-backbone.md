# Jamie Ko's design philosophy: the backbone of CCGL Journal

## Read this before choosing the visual treatment

This is the project's design backbone, not an optional polish checklist. Apply it before expanding scenes, generating art, adding motion or rewriting the interface. Read it alongside the [creative reference manifest](creative-reference-manifest.md), which supplies the current creative direction and production references. This guide governs why a design choice belongs; the manifest helps decide how to make it.

The principles below paraphrase the design lessons Bob adopted from his conversation with Jamie Ko. The CCGL-specific workflow is an application of those lessons, not a claim that Jamie authored or approved this game. Private conversation transcripts are intentionally not reproduced.

**Make something worth understanding, then make it possible to understand it.** A beautiful journal is not enough if the reader cannot follow the situation, see the consequences, or learn anything about the subject.

## The principles

1. **Learn the subject, not only the production tools.** Before visualizing leadership or collaboration, understand the relevant course material and how an insight follows from it. Separate a documented classroom concept, a personal interpretation and an invented story event. AI can help investigate and challenge ideas, but fluency and impressive output do not establish knowledge.

2. **Choose a clear point of view and a few connected threads.** Decide what this experience explores and what the reader should notice. Let evidence and believable character needs revise that direction. One, two or three threads are an editorial aid, not a requirement for three cards, three scenes or three slogans.

3. **Design for the person encountering it without us.** The journal must establish enough of the situation, people, stakes and next action to work without its creator standing beside it. Let a specific scene supply context when that is better than an introduction. Do not assume that a newcomer knows course terminology or our design vocabulary.

4. **Let structure explain.** Each beat should build from what came before and prepare what follows. Layout, sequence, proximity, scale and recurring objects should reveal relationships. Do not repair a missing story with more badges, category labels, explanatory panels or instructions. Use a precise label where it actually helps.

5. **Find originality in specific knowledge and observation.** Concrete wants, practical constraints, meaningful objects and considered interpretations carry more character than generic inspirational writing. Use real material only with appropriate permission and context. Invented characters and generated pictures must not be presented as documentary evidence.

6. **Make form carry meaning without shrinking ambition.** Painting, handwriting, colour, motion, silence and playful interaction are welcome. Each should contribute to understanding, feeling, atmosphere or action. The test is what it accomplishes, not whether it is technically impressive. Rich craft is compatible with clarity.

## Apply it to this journal

The existing prototype follows Mina, Jules and Dev through a campus proposal and remembers choices across later entries. Its situations and people are fictional; it is not a validated personality assessment. Those are the current materials, not an instruction to preserve every scene.

A useful working question is: **When a team makes a choice under pressure, what does that choice leave for someone else to carry?** Treat this as a proposed editorial spine to test against the story, not an established research finding or an unchangeable thesis.

Build the reader's route through changes in understanding:

- **Enter a situation:** show who wants what, the practical constraint and why the decision matters now.
- **Make a choice:** offer credible alternatives with different tradeoffs, not an obvious virtuous answer and two punishments.
- **Encounter a consequence:** let dialogue, an altered object, a task or a later entry show what changed because of the choice.
- **Reconsider:** reveal another person's perspective or a cost that was not initially visible.
- **Reflect:** help the reader articulate what they noticed in this situation without assigning a fixed identity or moral score.

This is a planning aid, not a five-panel interface template. A beat can do several jobs; some information can remain deliberately unresolved.

For example, a dotted phrase becoming ink can express commitment. A later handwritten revision can show a changed understanding. A recurring timer can make time pressure tangible. A colour bloom can express relief after a specific event. These are candidate mechanisms, not proof that the present build achieves those effects. Essential meaning and choices must remain available without animation, hover or sound.

## Working contract for Opus and other contributors

1. Inspect the current implementation and latest creative direction before editing. Coordinate with ongoing implementation work; prototype separately rather than overwriting it.
2. Write a short working brief: audience, subject question, intended experience, available source material, provisional point of view and main threads. Keep it in development notes, not as interface chrome.
3. For every important subject claim, record the source, interpretation, qualification and reason the reader needs it. If the workbook or relevant sources are unavailable, mark the gap rather than inventing a lesson.
4. Prototype the opening and one complete route using actual prose and representative art before multiplying pages or image variants. Explain how each scene changes the reader's understanding.
5. Give each major visual and interaction a narrative or experiential purpose. Preserve meaningful continuity while varying composition. Remove repeated explanations when the scene already communicates them.
6. Review three things separately: **substance**, **comprehension**, and **craft/function**. An attractive screenshot does not pass the first two; strong writing does not excuse broken interaction.
7. Test a fresh encounter: ask what is happening, what the person expects to do next, what changed after a choice and what supports the interpretation. Observe before explaining. Fix the earliest missing dependency, then recheck.
8. Verify mobile, keyboard, reduced-motion, revisit/rewrite and export states when changing them. Distinguish actual tests from planned tests and independent heuristic review from human audience research.

## Definition of a useful improvement

A revision should make a concrete situation, relationship, consequence or feeling more legible while preserving the journal's distinctive character. At handoff, identify:

- one subject insight and how it was derived;
- one structural or visual decision that helps the reader encounter it;
- one observed result from the actual artifact;
- any unresolved evidence or comprehension gap.

Do not equate more assets, more labels, more branches or more effects with progress. Keep the ambition; strengthen the substance and the route through it.
